Open Agent Index is for business owners deciding which self-hostable agent tools could handle a defined job in their company. It is not an agency directory. The index compares what each project documents about that work.

Five business functions
  • Customer support 31 tools
    Answering and routing customer questions. Ranked first: Dify.
  • Lead follow-up 19 tools
    Replying to and nurturing new enquiries. Ranked first: L Harness (line-harness-oss).
  • Scheduling and intake 10 tools
    Booking, forms and first-contact intake. Ranked first: n8n.
  • Back office 14 tools
    Invoices, documents and internal admin. Ranked first: n8n.
  • Reporting and content 22 tools
    Recurring reports and routine content. Ranked first (tied): AnythingLLM, Dify.
Five jobs owners hand off

Customer support: answer routine questions from approved help-centre material and send uncertain cases to a person.

Lead follow-up: sort a new enquiry, draft a useful reply, hold it for approval, and record the lead.

Scheduling and intake: offer available times, collect the information needed for the appointment, and confirm or reschedule it.

Back office: read supplier invoices, extract the important fields, and prepare entries for approval before anything is posted.

Reporting and content: turn a supplied dataset into a short, checked summary or draft that a person can approve before it goes out.

How to read a score

Read the score as a comparison of documented evidence for one function, not a guarantee of performance in your business.

The formula has four criteria, with the weights fixed in the scoring specification:

  • Research evidence, 35%: whether the project documents the job and whether independent reviews add evidence.
  • Owner fit, 30%: documented approval steps, self-host installation, data boundary, running cost, and community help.
  • Project health, 25%: release cadence and commit recency for the repository.
  • Licence clarity, 10%: whether the licence and the rights around the self-hosted code are clear.

“Dated” means the evidence was collected on the edition’s collection date. Repository health and tool facts can change after that date. Scores belong to their function and specification version, so each later edition is a new snapshot.

Licence classes

These are self-hostable agent tools, but their licences are not interchangeable. Open source is used only for tools labelled OSI-approved open source. Fair-code tools publish source while adding use conditions. Source-available tools publish source without meeting the OSI label used here. Mixed tools can contain more than one class, and the index keeps those distinctions visible.

The index publishes the sources behind its rows. If a value, source, or label looks wrong, use the correction path.
