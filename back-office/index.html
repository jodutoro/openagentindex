Back office: agent tools, ranked

Invoices, documents and internal admin.

Why this job, and what fails

Back-office automation earns its keep by taking repetition out of work that still needs a paper trail. The owner hands off a defined preparation job: read supplier invoices, pull out the vendor, date, total and tax, then prepare an entry for review. The system may route a record, attach the source document, or ask for missing information. It should not post the transaction merely because an extracted number looks tidy.

The failure that costs money is a bad entry that reaches the books. A missed tax amount, a transposed total, or a duplicate invoice can distort cash planning and create a reconciliation problem. Access is part of the risk too. An agent with write access to invoices, payments, or a ledger can do damage quickly if the workflow has no pause before the irreversible step.

Approval is therefore the first owner-fit question for this function. It should stop a prepared entry before posting and show the source document beside the proposed values. Data boundary is close behind: invoices contain supplier identities, bank details, addresses, and commercial terms. Setup burden matters because a back-office flow must handle file formats, OCR, permissions, retries, and an accounting-system connection. Running cost includes hosting, storage, OCR, and model use. Licence clarity matters when the workflow becomes part of the company's operating system, not just a trial.

Kestra's documented expense example pauses for manager approval before payment processing. n8n's template library includes an invoice validation and approval workflow, and Windmill documents flow approvals and agent-tool approval. Sim's finance page describes agents for invoice processing and reconciliation with approval routing, its QuickBooks integration reads and updates QuickBooks Online records, and its Human block can pause a run for a person to review or correct extracted data. ERPNext is an ERP for accounting, invoicing, procurement and stock; its AI route is the built-in MCP server in Frappe Framework v16, not a built-in assistant, and it lets an outside AI client find DocTypes and methods, read and change documents, and call whitelisted methods under the signed-in user's permissions, with no approval step documented for those writes. Invoice Ninja exposes invoice records through an API, but the collected evidence does not document a review step. Paperless-ngx documents document ingestion and optional AI suggestions, with those AI features disabled by default. These details describe different handoff points, not interchangeable features.

The evidence for this page shows documented job fit, owner-fit fields, repository health, licence classification and qualifying independent reviews. It does not show extraction accuracy on ten invoices, duplicate detection, accounting reconciliation, or whether an approval can be bypassed by a retry. The planned protocol will use sample supplier invoices and require every entry to post only after approval. Until then, a person should approve every value and every write.
