Knowledge base: agent tools, ranked

Answering staff questions from the business's own documents.

Why this job, and what fails

Every business keeps its answers in files. Pricing rules sit in a spreadsheet. The returns policy sits in a PDF. The steps for adding a supplier live in an old email. Staff lose time hunting for these, or they guess.

The job here is narrow: "Answer staff questions from the business's own internal documents, showing the document each answer came from." The asker is an employee, not a customer. A tool that only stores or indexes files has not done the job. It has to answer from those files, in plain words.

Two neighbouring jobs are separate. Replies to customers belong to customer support. Turning a supplied dataset into a summary or draft belongs to reporting and content. A chat window with no link to your documents belongs on neither page.

Citations matter because a wrong answer looks the same as a right one. When the tool names the document, a person can open it and read the line. Without that, staff must trust blindly or check everything, and the tool saves nothing. A cited answer also exposes old files. If the policy it points to is two years out of date, you see that at once.

Self-hosting here means the tool runs on a server you control, and your documents stay on that server. That is not the same as complete privacy. If the tool sends questions and excerpts to an outside model provider, those excerpts leave. Ask where the model runs.

Check four things before choosing.

First, which file types and sources it reads. These might be PDFs, Word files, spreadsheets, a shared drive, a wiki or email. A tool that cannot read your main source is no use to you, however neat it looks.

Second, who can see which documents. Payroll files and contracts should not answer a question from every employee. Look for permissions that follow the source.

Third, whether it shows the source with every answer, or only some of the time.

Fourth, what model it needs and what that costs to run. A hosted model bills by use. A local model needs a stronger server. Either way, the bill grows with the number of questions.
