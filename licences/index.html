Licence classes

What the Faronodo licence labels mean for a business that wants to self-host an AI tool.

Licence classes

  • OSI-approved open source (83) OSI-approved terms generally permit business use.
  • Fair-code (1) Free use with possible commercial limits.
  • Source-available (15) Readable code with additional conditions.
  • Mixed licence (14) Repository parts have different terms.

Collected 2026-11-19.

Each tool's licence chip is this site's dated classification of the terms found in its self-hosted repository. It tells you what to check before using the tool in your business. It is not legal advice. Read the current licence before deployment.

OSI-approved open source

The self-hosted code uses a licence approved by the Open Source Initiative. The Open Source Definition requires source code, permission for derived works and no restriction on business use.

What it means for your business

  • You can generally run it for your business and modify it, subject to the licence conditions.
  • Hosting it for a client or selling a service is not ruled out by this class. Check the exact licence, extensions, enterprise files and trademarks.
  • Review the version you deploy. Future releases can add separately licensed parts.

Mixed licence

Different parts of the self-hosted repository carry different terms. This can mean an open-source core with enterprise directories under a commercial licence, or several licence families in one repository.

What it means for your business

  • The core may be usable in your business while enterprise paths require a paid production licence.
  • Internal testing or modification may be allowed where production use, resale or managed hosting is restricted.
  • Check every directory, image, package and feature your deployment uses. Recheck them when you upgrade.

Source-available

The source code is readable, but the licence is not approved by the OSI. Additional conditions may limit competing services, multi-tenant use, branding changes or hosted delivery.

What it means for your business

  • Internal use and modification may be allowed, but the exact licence controls.
  • Resale, client work, hosted service or branding changes may be restricted. The indexed tools have different conditions.
  • Before going live, check the definitions of production, commercial use, competing use and hosted service.

Fair-code

Fair-code describes a software model, not one standard licence. The source is open and use is generally free, while authors place commercial restrictions on selling or commercializing the software.

What it means for your business

  • Use inside your company may be allowed. Hosting it for clients or selling access may require permission or a commercial agreement.
  • Modification and redistribution depend on the project's actual licence. Check its licence files and enterprise terms.
  • Treat each tool separately. Future releases may use different terms.
