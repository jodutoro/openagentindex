Lead follow-up starts at the moment an enquiry becomes easy to lose. The owner hands off the first response: read the web-form details, write a reply that uses the person's actual need, hold it for approval, send it once, and record the lead in the right place. That is a small workflow with several chances to create a mess. A generic greeting is not useful. Neither is a message sent twice because a webhook retried.

The money-losing failure is a missed or mistimed response. A lead who receives the wrong answer may never explain the problem again. A more dangerous version invents availability, pricing, or a promise that sales has not approved. Duplicate records and unlogged replies make the pipeline look healthier than it is, which hides the leak until someone checks the conversations by hand.

For this job, the approval check carries the most weight in practice. The person responsible for sales should see the draft before it leaves, especially when the enquiry includes a budget, a deadline, or a request outside the normal offer. The data-boundary check comes next. A lead form can contain phone numbers, company details, and free-text notes that should not be sent to every connected model or app. Setup burden also matters because the flow must survive retries, missing fields, and a CRM outage. Running cost matters when every form submission triggers enrichment or a model call.

n8n has documented agent tool approvals through chat channels. Twenty's Draft Email workflow step saves a generated email to the connected mailbox's Drafts folder for manual review instead of sending it, and Sim's Human block pauses a run until a person approves. Sim's HubSpot integration reads and updates CRM contacts and deals. EspoCRM captures leads through web forms or its API and converts them to accounts, contacts or opportunities; round-robin or least-busy assignment and its follow-up emails and tasks run from Workflows, which need the paid Advanced Pack extension, while its AI rests on a free official MCP extension for which the evidence records no approval step. Krayin CRM's Magic AI reads an uploaded PDF and creates a pre-filled draft lead, and its developer docs list image OCR as planned: that is lead capture, not follow-up messaging. Typebot is useful as a conversational qualification front end, but the collected evidence does not document an approve-before-send step. Activepieces documents lead enrichment and connected actions, while its Flow Approvals evidence is marked paid-plan only. Those are materially different claims. A human should still approve the wording, recipient, offer, and send event, then review whether the CRM record contains the source and the final message.

The evidence covers documented workflow capability, owner-fit fields, repository signals, licence clarity and independent reviews where they passed the method's rules. It does not prove response quality, delivery rates, duplicate protection, CRM reconciliation, or conversion. No hands-on lead has been submitted to these tools for this edition.
